Hypospadias in Children

A concise evidence-based overview of hypospadias assessment, surgical goals, complications, long-term follow-up, and what remains uncertain.

Educational overview. This page does not provide medical advice for an individual child.

Clinical overview

Hypospadias is a congenital condition in which the urethral opening (meatus) lies on the underside of the penis, short of its usual position at the tip of the glans. It is often accompanied by ventral penile curvature (chordee) and an abnormal distribution of foreskin.

Hypospadias is not defined by meatal location alone. Severity and surgical complexity depend on the whole penile anatomy, including curvature, the quality of the urethral plate and glans, penile size, and the tissue available for repair. This is the point of the Academy episode “Hypospadias Is Not a Meatal Diagnosis”.

Treatment is surgical. Its timing and technique are individualized, and no single technique is best for every child.

What clinicians assess

Meatal location
Where the meatus lies. This describes one feature of the anatomy and does not by itself determine severity or complexity.
Ventral curvature
Whether the penis curves ventrally and whether the curvature needs to be corrected. Curvature is assessed separately from meatal location.
Urethral plate
The quality and appearance of the plate of tissue leading to the meatus.
Glans
The size and configuration of the glans, which bears on glans closure.
Penile size
Penile size, which influences the tissue available and the repair.
Foreskin and available tissue
The foreskin and other local tissue that can be used in repair.
Prior surgery
Whether a repair has already been done, and the condition of the tissue afterward.

Meatal location, curvature, penile anatomy, and tissue quality

These features are considered together. The meatus is the most visible finding, but curvature, plate quality, glans size, penile size, and tissue quality each affect the repair.

For this reason a distal meatus does not make a repair simple, and a proximal meatus raises further questions, such as whether repair is done in one stage or two. Both are discussed in the Academy (“Distal Hypospadias: The Myth of the Simple Repair” and “Proximal Hypospadias: One Stage or Two?”).

Primary and reoperative hypospadias

Primary repair is the first operation. Reoperative (redo) repair follows an earlier repair, often in scarred tissue and altered anatomy, so the available tissue and the anatomy differ from the primary setting. Redo cases are assessed individually, and this page makes no general statement about expected results.

Surgical goals

The goals of repair include a urethral opening at or near the tip of the glans, a straight penis, and a urethra that supports a normal urinary stream, together with an acceptable appearance.

Appearance is one goal and not the only one. Function matters, including urinary stream and, later in life, sexual and reproductive considerations. Whether the right outcomes are being measured is discussed in the Academy (“Cosmetic versus Functional Success”).

Complications and long-term follow-up

Problems that clinicians watch for after repair include urethrocutaneous fistula, narrowing of the urethra or meatus, residual or recurrent curvature, and problems with wound healing. This page does not quote complication rates.

Follow-up extends beyond the early postoperative period, because urinary function, appearance, and sexual and reproductive function are assessed over time, including into adulthood (Academy: “Long-Term Adult Outcomes After Childhood Hypospadias Repair”).

What remains uncertain

  • how to define and measure success beyond appearance
  • long-term urinary, sexual, and reproductive outcomes into adulthood
  • one-stage versus staged repair in proximal hypospadias
  • the best timing of surgery for an individual child
  • which repairs are at risk of failure, and why
  • how best to protect the repair against fistula
  • how to approach redo surgery when tissue quality is poor
  • the role of preoperative hormonal treatment
